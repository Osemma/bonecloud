The Problem

The industry's most expensive exhaust

Even with liquid cooling, immersion, and rear-door heat exchangers, every data centre ends the same way: gigawatt-hours of low-grade heat rejected to the atmosphere, and a separate electrical bill paid to move it there.

The Solution

Heat-driven hybrid cooling

A closed thermodynamic loop that uses the facility's own waste heat to generate cooling — eliminating the electrical compression stage entirely.

Recovering heat at this temperature range sounds simple. It isn't! Conventional recovery systems need higher-grade sources. That's the engineering problem we solved.

01
Heat capture
Low-grade waste heat from the data centre's coolant return loop is captured at the source.
02
Thermal compression
The captured heat drives a specialised compressor that powers a heat pump cycle.
03
Cooling delivery
The heat pump produces chilled fluid for the DC loop while concentrating rejected heat — exportable for district heating or air-rejected at a fraction of conventional fan power.

Impact

Why it matters

Approaching unity PUE
Cooling energy approaches zero relative to IT load.
Approximately zero WUE
No cooling tower or adiabatic cooler evaporation.
Reduced CUE
Lower electrical draw for cooling directly reduces Carbon Usage Effectiveness and Scope 2 emissions across the facility.
Concentrated heat output
Heat rejected by the condenser is concentrated to a level suitable for district heating export or efficient air-side rejection.
Density-adaptive
Higher rack power means higher waste heat temperature, which improves the driving potential of the cycle. Performance scales with the problem.
Noise elimination
Routing rejected heat to district loops or low-fan-power air-side rejection removes the broadband mechanical noise of conventional cooling plant entirely.
Installation Compatibility

Where it fits

Designed for integration with existing infrastructure — not as a replacement for it.

Liquid cooling & direct-to-chip
Integrates with rear-door heat exchangers, cold plates, and direct-to-chip loops where return temperatures sit in the recoverable band.
Condenser-side replacement
Sits on the condenser side of existing chillers — replaces cooling towers without disturbing the primary plant.
Modular retrofit
Attaches to the existing hydronic loop without requiring full plant replacement. Phased deployment, minimal downtime.
Hyperscale to edge
Scales across hyperscale, colocation, and edge deployments. Single skid for edge sites; arrayed modules for large facilities.
